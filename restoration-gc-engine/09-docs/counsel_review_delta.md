# Compliance Lexicon — Changes Since Counsel's Sign-Off

**Purpose of this document:** counsel reviewed and approved the compliance lexicon and disclaimers (README deployment step 8). Since that approval, the lexicon has been modified multiple times — including twice in the course of preparing this very deployment. This document is the honest, complete diff: what changed, why, and whether it touches legal substance or just detection accuracy. **This is not a substitute for counsel actually looking at it** — no tool available in this build can obtain real legal sign-off — but it's built so a lawyer can confirm the changes in minutes rather than re-reading the whole lexicon from scratch.

## What did NOT change

- Every required-disclaimer's **text** (`tx_disclaimer`, `fl_deadline_disclaimer`, `ok_notice`, `tx_contract_notice`) is byte-identical to what counsel originally reviewed. No disclaimer wording, no citation, no statutory reference was edited.
- The banned categories themselves are unchanged: no outcome guarantees, no deductible-waiver inducements, no legal advice, no paid public-adjuster referral in IL, no unauthorized-adjusting language. Nothing was added to or removed from this list.
- The underlying legal positions and citations (TX Ins. Code §707.002/§4102.163, TX Bus. & Com. Code §27.02(b), OK 59 O.S. §1151.30, FL Stat. §627.70132, IL 215 ILCS 5/155.51, IL DOI Bulletin 2026-02) are unchanged.

## What DID change — 6 items, all found by actually executing the rules against real/live text rather than reading them

| # | Rule | What changed | Detection-precision fix, or does it touch legal substance? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `us_no_guarantee` | `\b100%\b` never matched "100% of homeowners..." (a regex word-boundary quirk: `%` and a following space are both non-word characters, so `\b` can't find a boundary there). Added a lookahead alternative. | **Precision only.** Strictly catches more of what was already banned; catches nothing new in kind. |
| 2 | `tx_no_deductible` / `ok_no_deductible` | Original pattern required near-adjacency to "deductible" and missed realistic phrasing ("waive **your** deductible"). Rewritten to require "we" as the explicit subject, with a wider gap. | **Precision only**, in the direction of catching more real violations, not fewer. |
| 3 | `il_no_deductible` (fix 1 of 2) | Gap of `.{0,15}` was too narrow for phrasing like "inflate the estimate to cover your deductible"; widened to `.{0,40}`. | **Precision only.** Catches more of what was already banned. |
| 4 | `us_no_legal_advice` | The bare "legal advice" alternative matched the *compliant* required disclaimer's own negated phrasing ("does not ... provide legal advice"), meaning the TX disclaimer this system requires would perpetually re-trigger the rule it's supposed to satisfy. Narrowed to an affirmative-offer construction; also broadened "bad faith case" to catch "bad-faith lawsuit" phrasing. | **Precision only**, correcting a self-contradiction (the gate would have blocked its own required output). Doesn't change what's banned. |
| 5 | `tx_contract_notice` | Added an `applies_to` column (`content` vs. `contract`); this rule is now excluded from the automated Compliance-Gate Agent's check of `content_drafts`, since that table never holds actual signed contracts (those are generated via DocuSign, outside this pipeline). | **Procedural, not substantive** — but the most legally significant of these six. The disclaimer text and citation are unchanged, and this rule was never correctly enforceable against marketing content in the first place (it was a false-positive machine before this fix, flagging every TX LinkedIn post). What changed is *which control* is responsible: contract-template review is now explicitly a **manual, counsel-driven** control rather than something the automated gate touches at all. **Recommend counsel explicitly confirm this division of responsibility is acceptable** — i.e., that manual review of the TX §27.02(b) notice at contract-template creation time (not per-content-item) is the control they're relying on. |
| 6 | `il_no_deductible` (fix 2 of 2, done in this pass) | Unlike `tx_no_deductible`/`ok_no_deductible`, this rule had no first-person/offer subject scoping — it fired on *any* mention of waiving/absorbing/inflating within 40 characters of "deductible," including compliant third-party statements like "Illinois law prohibits a contractor from waiving your deductible." This would have blocked the system's own required IL educational content (the loaded content calendar's Day 22 post is literally "IL deductible-fraud statute explained"). Added the same `we(?:'ll\| will\| can\| could\| may)?` subject requirement TX/OK already use. | **Narrows automated enforcement**, the one item on this list where that's true in a way worth naming plainly: content that previously would have been auto-flagged for rewrite now passes. The narrowing only excludes third-person/explanatory statements about the law — an actual first-person offer ("we'll waive your deductible") still matches and still blocks. Verified with 2 new red-team fixtures (`08-testing/compliance_redteam.md` #45–46, both re-confirmed live). **Recommend counsel spot-check this one specifically**, since it's the item most directly changing what the automated gate will and won't catch. |

## Bottom line

Five of six changes are corrections that make the automated gate more faithful to the policy counsel already approved (catch more real violations, stop self-flagging compliant required text). One (`tx_contract_notice`) is a scope/procedure clarification with no text change. One (`il_no_deductible` fix 2) is a genuine narrowing of automated coverage, scoped specifically to exclude educational/third-person statements about the law — which is also exactly the category of content this system is built to produce.

**Recommended action:** send this document to counsel for a targeted confirmation (not a full re-review) before the next production send, specifically on items #5 and #6. All 6 changes are also individually documented with full context in `08-testing/compliance_redteam.md` and `09-docs/risk_register.md` (#17, #18, #22) if a deeper look is wanted.
